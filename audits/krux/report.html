Quantum-vulnerable Pedersen commitment: threshold signing and MPC protocols (4 file(s))

Long-term post-quantum riskConfidence: Medium

Analyst review (QSI review, 2026-10-29): confirmed by the analyst. Correct but narrow, and the narrowness is worth recording so nobody reads this as four more rows of spending exposure. All four are Liquid, not Bitcoin: vendor/embit/src/embit/liquid/pset.py:101 and liquid/transaction.py:90 call secp256k1.pedersen_commit for confidential-transaction value commitments, via util/secp256k1.py:51 and ctypes_secp256k1.py:949. The property at risk is the commitment's *binding*, which rests on the discrete log, so a CRQC could forge a value commitment; the *hiding* property is information-theoretic and no quantum computer touches it. A post-quantum signature standard does not fix this — it needs a different commitment scheme — and it only matters to users who spend Liquid assets.

15 reference(s) to Pedersen commitment across 4 file(s), e.g. vendor/embit/src/embit/liquid/pset.py:101. Role, judged from paths, function names and nearby code: threshold signing and MPC protocols. Paillier, Pedersen commitments and the discrete-logarithm proofs inside threshold ECDSA and EdDSA protocols all fall to Shor's algorithm; a post-quantum threshold scheme is a protocol change, not a parameter change.

Potential cryptographic riskConfidence: Medium

Analyst review (QSI review, 2026-10-29): confirmed by the analyst. Real and deliberate: src/krux/kef.py defines the Krux Encrypted Format, and of its eleven versions four are ECB — version 0 "AES-ECB v1" (released 2023.08 for BIP-39 entropy), 5 "AES-ECB", 6 "AES-ECB +p" and 7 "AES-ECB +c". Reported at medium rather than high, because the usual reasons for calling ECB high do not all hold here and saying so is the point of a review. Every version carries an `auth` field sizing an authentication tag, so integrity is not left to the mode; GCM versions 20 and 21 exist in the same table; and the maintainers document the actual hazard themselves, version 6 reading "safe padding: for mid-sized plaintext w/o duplicate blocks" and 7 "may compact otherwise duplicate blocks". For the intended payload — one or two blocks of high-entropy mnemonic — ECB's block-repetition leak has little to bite on. The recommendation is not "remove ECB" (that would orphan 2023 backups) but to keep the ECB versions for decryption of old backups and stop offering them for new ones. automated severity: High.
